Introduction

About

I am a PhD researcher in Mechanical Engineering at the University of Genova, working at the intersection of mechanical systems and artificial intelligence. My research centers on condition-based monitoring and AI-driven fault detection — using vibration and acoustic signal analysis, spectrogram-based classification, and convolutional neural networks to identify defects in industrial components such as powered window regulators.

I hold a Master's degree in Energy Systems Engineering from the U.S.–Pakistan Centre for Advanced Studies in Energy (NUST) and a Bachelor's degree in Mechanical Engineering from UET Peshawar. This background lets me move between classical mechanical and thermal engineering and modern data-driven methods.

Before starting my PhD, I spent six years as a Lecturer and Head of Department at the University of Technology, Nowshera, teaching mechanical and energy engineering courses and coordinating academic and outcome-based education processes. My publications span AI in mechanical systems, renewable energy, materials science, and smart energy systems.

Selected Work

Research Projects

PhD Research · 2023–Present

AI-Based Fault Detection in Powered Window Regulators

An end-to-end system classifying products OK / NOK from vibration and current signals captured during production.

  • Signal acquisition via accelerometers and DAQ, with filtering, normalization, and segmentation
  • STFT-based conversion of signals into spectrograms
  • CNN architectures (EfficientNet, VGG-based) and embedding models (YAMNet)
  • Evaluated via accuracy, precision/recall, ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, and confusion matrix
Ongoing Implementation

Smart DAQ + AI Integration System

A real-time system connecting vibration data acquisition to AI classification and industrial control.

  • Trigger-based acquisition synchronized to machine cycles
  • TensorFlow inference within the acquisition pipeline
  • OPC UA communication with the production-line PLC, supporting automated actuation
Master's Thesis · NUST

Techno-Economic Analysis of Parabolic Trough Solar Thermal Power Plants

A feasibility study of solar thermal power generation under Pakistan's climatic conditions — system design and modeling of parabolic trough collectors, simulation with TRNSYS / SAM, and cost, efficiency, and payback-period analysis.

Open Source

Software Projects

Desktop tools built alongside my research — mostly to solve problems that came up while working with DAQ recordings, documents, and data pipelines.

Python · Tkinter

Markdown Converter

A free, open-source Windows desktop app that converts PDF, Word, PowerPoint, Excel, HTML, and plain text into clean Markdown, using Microsoft's MarkItDown library. Markdown is far more token-efficient for LLM workflows than raw document formats, so converting first stretches a model's context window further — whether for a RAG pipeline or pasting a document into a chat.

View source on GitHub ↗
Python · PyQt5 · h5py

HDF5 Player

A desktop app for playing vibration and current signals from HDF5 acquisition files as audio, built to accelerate signal inspection during DAQ-based research at the Dynamo Lab, DIME, University of Genova. The recordings' 22,050 Hz sample rate maps directly onto standard audio playback, making it possible to audibly detect anomalies, clipping, and noise that are harder to spot on a waveform plot alone.

View source on GitHub ↗
Python · pdfplumber

PDF to Excel Converter

A free Windows desktop app that extracts transaction tables from bank statement PDFs into a properly formatted .xlsx file. The extraction engine derives column boundaries from the transaction data itself rather than assuming a fixed bank layout, so it generalizes across statement formats.
